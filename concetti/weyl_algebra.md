# Weyl Algebra

## Contenuto

Forma esponenziata (bounded) della CCR: gli operatori di Weyl aggirano l'ostruzione di Wintner (che vale per operatori limitati non esponenziati) ma soddisfano relazioni non commutative equivalenti a $[\hat Q,\hat P]=i\hbar I$ per differenziazione formale. Genera la C*-algebra di Weyl, su cui poi si costruisce la quantizzazione di Weyl (§4.4).

---

## Testo LaTeX pronto per la tesi (§2.3)

```latex
\begin{definition}[Weyl operators and the Weyl algebra]\label{def:weyl-algebra}
For $(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^2$, the \emph{Weyl operators} on $\mathcal{H}=L^2(\mathbb{R})$
are the unitaries
\[
W(q,p) := e^{i(p\hat Q - q\hat P)/\hbar},
\]
with $\hat Q,\hat P$ the (unbounded, self-adjoint) position and momentum operators.
They satisfy the \emph{Weyl relations}
\[
W(q_1,p_1)\,W(q_2,p_2) = e^{-\tfrac{i}{2\hbar}(q_1p_2-q_2p_1)}\,W(q_1+q_2,p_1+p_2).
\]
The \emph{Weyl algebra} $\mathcal{A}_W$ is the C*-algebra generated by
$\{W(q,p):(q,p)\in\mathbb{R}^2\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The Weyl relations are the bounded (exponentiated) form of the canonical commutation
relation $[\hat Q,\hat P]=i\hbar I$, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:wintner} cannot be
realised by bounded operators. Formally differentiating the Weyl relations at
$q_1=p_1=q_2=p_2=0$ recovers $[\hat Q,\hat P]=i\hbar I$ on a common dense domain.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Stone–von Neumann]\label{thm:stone-von-neumann}
Up to unitary equivalence, $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ carries a unique irreducible
representation of the Weyl relations for which $(q,p)\mapsto W(q,p)$ is strongly
continuous.
\end{theorem}
```
