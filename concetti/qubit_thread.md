## Purpose

A single physical system — spin-½ — traced through all four chapters of the thesis. Its purpose is to show that the four mathematical perspectives are not independent theories but four views of the same object.

---

## The four Qubits at a glance

| Qubit | Chapter | Mathematics | Physical interpretation |
|-------|---------|-------------|------------------------|
| **I** | §1.6 | (S², ω_{S²}) as symplectic manifold; {xᵢ,xⱼ} = εᵢⱼₖ xₖ | Classical spin phase space |
| **II** | §2.5 | M₂(ℂ) as non-commutative C*-algebra; [σᵢ,σⱼ] = 2iεᵢⱼₖ σₖ | Quantum observable algebra |
| **III** | §3.6 | ℙℂ¹ ≅ S² as symplectic manifolds (same ω, up to normalization) | Quantum state space = classical phase space |
| **IV** | §4.6 | Dirac condition exact on generators xᵢ; coherent state convergence | Algebraic bridge confirmed |

---

## The closed loop

- **Qubit I → II**: same Lie algebra 𝔰𝔲(2), two representations (Poisson vs. commutator)
- **Qubit II → III**: the state space of M₂(ℂ) is the same manifold as the phase space of Qubit I
- **Qubit III → IV**: the symplectic identity is not just geometric but algebraic — Dirac condition holds exactly on the generators
- **Qubit IV → I**: by coherent state convergence, the quantum fibre returns to the classical fibre

## 🔴 Open issues in the thread

### C1 — Normalization discrepancy (Qubit III, §3.6)

- ω_FS on ℙℂ¹: **½ sinθ dθ∧dφ**
- ω_{S²} in Qubit I: **sinθ dθ∧dφ**
- Factor of 2. **Unresolved.**

**Best resolution** (consistent with Qubit IV): use spin-j conventions. For the spin-½ system, the classical phase space S² should carry the form ω_{S²}^{(1/2)} = ½ sinθ dθ∧dφ (area 2π, corresponding to j=½). Then ω_FS = ω_{S²}^{(1/2)} and the identification is exact.

### C2 — Exactness overstated (Qubit IV, §4.6)

The Dirac condition holds *exactly* on the generators {xᵢ}, but only *asymptotically* on general f ∈ C^∞(S²). The text must make this distinction explicit.

## Testo LaTeX pronto per la tesi (§1.6, §2.5, §3.6, §4.6)

Nota: i blocchi seguenti risolvono gli issue aperti C1 (discrepanza di normalizzazione) e C2 (esattezza della condizione di Dirac) incorporando direttamente la soluzione nel testo, invece di lasciarli come problemi aperti.

```latex
% --- §1.6, Qubit I ---
\begin{example}[Qubit I — classical spin phase space]\label{ex:qubit-1}
Let $M=S^2=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^3:|x|=\tfrac12\}$ with symplectic form
$\omega_{S^2}^{(1/2)}=\tfrac12\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ (normalised to
total area $2\pi$, i.e.\ to spin $j=\tfrac12$). The coordinate functions
$x_1,x_2,x_3$ satisfy
\[
\{x_i,x_j\} = \varepsilon_{ijk}\,x_k.
\]
This is the phase space of a classical spin-$\tfrac12$ system, and
$(C^\infty(S^2),\{\cdot,\cdot\})$ realises the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$
(Definition~\ref{def:lie-algebra}) via the Poisson bracket.
\end{example}

% --- §2.5, Qubit II ---
\begin{example}[Qubit II — quantum spin observable algebra]\label{ex:qubit-2}
Let $\mathcal{A}=M_2(\mathbb{C})$, a non-commutative C*-algebra
(Definition~\ref{def:c-star-algebra}) generated by the self-adjoint Pauli matrices
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$, satisfying
\[
[\sigma_i,\sigma_j] = 2i\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\,\sigma_k.
\]
$M_2(\mathbb{C})$ realises the same Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ as
Example~\ref{ex:qubit-1}, via the commutator rather than the Poisson bracket.
\end{example}

% --- §3.6, Qubit III ---
\begin{example}[Qubit III — geometric closure]\label{ex:qubit-3}
For $\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^2$, $\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{CP}^1$ is
diffeomorphic to $S^2$, and under this identification the Fubini–Study form of
Proposition~\ref{prop:PH-kahler} coincides with $\omega_{S^2}^{(1/2)}$ of
Example~\ref{ex:qubit-1}: quantum state space and classical phase space are, for the
qubit, \emph{the same symplectic manifold}, up to the spin-$\tfrac12$ normalisation
fixed above.
\end{example}

% --- §4.6, Qubit IV ---
\begin{example}[Qubit IV — exactness of the Dirac condition]\label{ex:qubit-4}
Restricted to the linear generators $x_1,x_2,x_3\in C^\infty(S^2)$ of
Example~\ref{ex:qubit-1}, the strict deformation quantization of
Chapter~\ref{cap:deformation-quantization} satisfies Dirac's condition
(Definition~\ref{def:rieffel-sdq}(b)) \emph{exactly}, with no $\hbar\to0$ limit
required:
\[
\frac{1}{i\hbar}\big[\widehat{x_i},\widehat{x_j}\big] = \widehat{\{x_i,x_j\}}
\qquad \text{for all } \hbar.
\]
This exactness is special to the linear generators, which close the finite-dimensional
Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(2)$; for a general $f\in C^\infty(S^2)$, Dirac's condition
holds only asymptotically as $\hbar\to0$ (cf.\ Theorem~\ref{thm:gvh}), the classical
limit being recovered via $SU(2)$ coherent-state convergence.
\end{example}
```
